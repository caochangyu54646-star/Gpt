# Animation and illustration analysis

Use for anime, hand-drawn animation, motion comics, illustrated films, painted animation, stylized 3D, and mixed-media animation.

## 1. Rendering family

Identify visible operations rather than stopping at a genre label:

- line-led cel rendering
- painted, gouache, watercolor, ink, or paper-textured rendering
- vector-clean motion design
- stylized 3D with 2D treatment
- collage, cutout, or mixed media
- hybrid characters and backgrounds using different systems

Record whether edges come from contours, value changes, color boundaries, brush marks, lighting, or depth separation.

## 2. Character system

Analyze:

- silhouette and body proportion
- head-to-body relationship and exaggeration
- face-family construction and identity variation
- eyes, brows, nose, mouth, ears, hands, joints, and anatomy simplification
- contour hierarchy, line weight, tapering, breaks, and internal lines
- hair masses versus strands
- clothing shape, folds, edge behavior, and material cues
- local color, complexion, accent hierarchy, and shadow colors
- age, role, personality, and body-type conditionals
- paper, brush, bleed, grain, line boil, or digital cleanliness

Do not convert one character's body, complexion, costume, or facial feature into a global rule. Extract the system that produces different identities.

## 3. Background and space system

Analyze:

- accurate perspective versus deliberate flattening
- large shape grouping and silhouette rhythm
- foreground, middle-ground, and background responsibilities
- detail density and edge sharpness by depth
- scale references, path, occlusion, reveal, and negative space
- architecture and natural-form simplification
- painted softness, optical blur, atmospheric haze, or omitted information
- palette width, value range, saturation, accents, and lighting abstraction
- texture scale and environmental motion

Do not call every soft background shallow depth of field. Identify whether softness is optical, painterly, atmospheric, or informational.

## 4. Color and lighting systems

Separate:

- local color from illumination color
- character palette from environment palette
- stable palette logic from time, weather, emotion, or location variants
- light direction from graphic shadow design
- highlight shapes, shadow families, rim treatment, glow, and atmosphere
- value grouping used for readability

When lighting is stylized rather than physical, describe its graphical rules without pretending it is realistic optics.

## 5. Material and effects systems

For materials, identify how wood, stone, metal, fabric, skin, foliage, water, smoke, or other recurring families are simplified and textured.

For stylized effects, define:

- source and attachment
- shape language and edge behavior
- color hierarchy
- propagation and deformation
- interaction with characters and backgrounds
- frame cadence, residue, and decay

Do not let effects become disconnected overlays unless that separation is visibly part of the style.

## 6. Character-background coexistence

Measure relative properties:

- character edge sharpness versus background softness
- character detail density versus environmental detail
- outline contrast versus environmental contour contrast
- value and saturation separation
- shared light direction and palette
- overlap, silhouette readability, contact, and depth
- texture compatibility or deliberately controlled difference

Write operational coexistence rules, not “the character blends naturally.”

## 7. Motion and temporal behavior

When clips or sequential frames exist, inspect:

- frame cadence and held drawings
- key-pose emphasis and in-between density
- line and texture boil
- limited versus full animation
- secondary motion in hair, cloth, smoke, foliage, light, and effects
- smear frames, impact frames, arcs, deformation, and recovery
- camera movement versus drawn-background movement
- parallax layer count and speed
- motion blur and focus transitions
- temporal texture and color stability

With stills only, label any video extension inferred or proposed.

## 8. Common failure shields

- Do not use “anime,” “Chinese style,” or “storybook” as a complete definition.
- Do not force characters and backgrounds into one rendering system when the references separate them.
- Do not turn background softness into indiscriminate blur.
- Do not make every face identical.
- Do not use random anatomy lines that read as scars.
- Do not copy reference costumes, objects, locations, or actions into a content-free prompt.
- Do not describe graphical lighting as verified physical lighting.
